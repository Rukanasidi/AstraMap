Mapped hazards & uncertainty

Hazards in this prototype: steep slopes and rough (blocky) terrain, both derived from the HiRISE DTM. Individual boulders, soft sand and rock-abundance maps are not modelled; roughness is only a proxy. Terrain is an 8 m average of a 2 m DTM, so features smaller than a few metres are invisible to the planner. No-data cells are never treated as safe.

Methods

Route: A* on an 8-connected grid; Dijkstra is the baseline. Edge cost = 3-D distance × (1 + k·(s/limit)² + kr·(rough/limit)²), s = |Δz| / horizontal step. Moves with s above the limit, cells rougher than the roughness limit, and any no-data cell are excluded. The A* heuristic is straight-line distance (never overestimates, since the multiplier ≥ 1), so A* and Dijkstra return equal cost.

Roughness: RMS of the 2 m DTM elevation minus its ~8 m Gaussian smooth, averaged over each 8 m cell. It flags blocky or broken terrain, not individual rocks.

Slope layer: central-difference gradient of the 8 m DTM (display); the planner uses move-to-move slopes.

Oxygen estimate: metabolic watts × time ÷ 20.1 kJ/L. Assumption-driven.

Limitations: 8 m smoothing hides rocks and ripples; no boulder, sand-softness or traction model; no thermal, dust or comms model; thresholds shown are from the team's reference document and should be re-checked against NASA-STD-3001 before use.
